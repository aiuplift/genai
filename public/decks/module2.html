01
How LLMs Generate Text
Understanding the engine so you can steer it
Transition slide. In Module 1 we said AI is 'fancy autocomplete.' Now let's go one level deeper — understanding the five-step pipeline gives you intuition for WHY certain prompting techniques work.

The Five-Step Pipeline

1️⃣
Tokenization — Your input is split into tokens (word fragments). "Unbelievable" → ["un", "believ", "able"]
2️⃣
Embeddings — Each token becomes a vector (coordinates in meaning-space). Similar concepts cluster together.
3️⃣
Attention — The transformer decides which tokens relate to which. Connects "said" back to "manager," not "Tuesday."
4️⃣
Prediction — Produces a probability distribution for the next token. Picks one, adds it, repeats.
5️⃣
Temperature — Controls randomness. Low = predictable/safe. High = creative/risky.
Your prompt sets the prediction context. Better input tokens → stronger patterns → better output.
Don't dwell on each step too long. The key takeaway: your prompt is the steering wheel. The five-step pipeline explains WHY specific, well-structured prompts produce better output — they give the model stronger statistical patterns to work with. Temperature explains why AI can be creative OR precise, depending on settings.

Context Windows & Token Limits

📐 WHAT IS A CONTEXT WINDOW?
The total amount of text (input + output) the model can "see" at once. Think of it as the model's working memory.

Anything outside this window doesn't exist to the model.
📊 TYPICAL SIZES (2025)
GPT-4o: ~128K tokens (~100 pages)
Claude 3.5: ~200K tokens (~150 pages)
Gemini 1.5: ~1M tokens (~750 pages)

1 token ≈ 0.75 words (English)
"The context window is your conversation's RAM. When it fills up, earlier information gets pushed out — and the model 'forgets.'"
Context windows matter for practical prompt engineering. If you're working with long documents or multi-turn conversations, you'll hit limits. Key insight: the model doesn't remember previous conversations unless you paste them in. Each session starts fresh. This is why templates and structured prompts are essential — they pack maximum useful context into minimum tokens.

Why does a clear, specific prompt produce better AI output than a vague one?
A The AI tries harder when you write more
B It gives the model stronger patterns to predict from
C Clear prompts bypass the AI's safety filters
D The AI searches a database more accurately
Interactive quiz. Answer B is correct. This connects back to the pipeline — your input tokens set the prediction context. Vague prompts give weak statistical signals; specific prompts give strong ones. Let participants discuss briefly before revealing.
02
The CRAFT Framework
A repeatable structure for effective prompts
Transition slide. Now we move from theory to practice. CRAFT is a mnemonic that gives participants a repeatable structure for writing prompts. It works for any AI tool, any task, any industry.

The CRAFT Framework

C
Context
Background info & situation
R
Role
Who the AI should be
A
Action
What to do + constraints
F
Format
Shape of the output
T
Tone
Voice & formality level
Every great prompt has at least 3 of these 5 elements. The best prompts use all 5.
Introduce the CRAFT mnemonic. Ask participants to think of their most recent AI prompt — how many of these five elements did it include? Most people include only Action (the command). Adding Context and Role dramatically improves output quality.

Weak vs Strong Prompt

❌ WEAK PROMPT
Write me an email asking for a pay raise.
⚠️ No role, no context, no constraints, no format, no tone guidance. The AI has to guess everything.
✅ STRONG PROMPT (CRAFT)
[R] You are a career coach specialising in tech compensation.
[C] I'm a senior engineer, 4 years at company, led 2 launches.
[A] Write a 200-word email requesting a compensation review.
[F] Open with appreciation, present metrics, close with meeting request.
[T] Confident but collegial. Do not invent accomplishments.
Same task, dramatically different output quality. The strong prompt channels the prediction engine precisely.
Side-by-side comparison. The weak prompt gives the model almost nothing — it'll produce generic, template-sounding output. The strong prompt specifies every CRAFT element and includes a critical guardrail ('do not invent accomplishments'). Ask: which output would you actually send to your manager?

Constraint-Based Prompting

🎯 WHAT TO CHANGE
"Rewrite the opening to be more direct" is better than "improve this."
🔒 WHAT TO KEEP
"Keep the original deadline of March 15th. Don't change the greeting."
🚫 WHAT TO AVOID
"Do not add commitments. Avoid jargon the client wouldn't understand."
🛡️ The Anchor Guardrail
"Do not invent policies, reasons, or details that I have not provided."
This single sentence prevents the most dangerous AI failure: confident fabrication.
Constraints are the secret weapon of professional prompt engineers. Without boundaries, AI drifts — adding invented context, shifting tone, making unauthorized promises. Three categories: what to change, what to keep, what to avoid. The anchor guardrail should be in every professional prompt.
🧠 QUICK CHECK
Which element is MISSING from this prompt? "Write a professional email declining a meeting invitation."
A Action — it doesn't say what to do
B Format — it doesn't specify length
C Tone — it doesn't define voice
D All of the above (Context, Role, Format, Tone)
Interactive quiz. Answer D is correct — the prompt only has an Action element. It's missing Context (who, what relationship), Role (what expertise to draw on), Format (length, structure), and Tone (formal vs casual). This is the typical 'lazy prompt' that produces generic output.
03
AI Writing Failure Patterns
Learn to spot what goes wrong — before you hit send
Transition slide. Even with good prompts, AI output can fail in predictable ways. Learning these patterns turns participants into effective editors of AI-generated content. This section is about building critical evaluation skills.

Four Failure Patterns to Watch For

🎭
Wrong Assumptions
AI fills gaps in your prompt with invented context. It assumes the complaint was about timing when it was about quality.
🎵
Tone Mismatches
Over-softens feedback until the message is lost. Over-formalises casual team comms. Dilutes urgency with politeness padding.
🔮
Invented Details
Adds fabricated dates, policies, statistics. "As per our 90-day review policy..." — you never mentioned that policy.
✨
Over-Polishing
Excessive em-dashes, lists of three, hedging phrases. Grammatically perfect but utterly generic. Lost your authentic voice.
Every AI draft needs human review. These four patterns tell you exactly what to look for.
Four predictable failure modes. Wrong Assumptions: the AI fills gaps you didn't provide. Tone Mismatches: technically correct but wrong for the situation. Invented Details: the most dangerous — fabricated facts that sound authoritative. Over-Polishing: the AI 'voice' that sounds like everyone and no one. Ask: which have you experienced?

The 60-Second Verification Checklist

✓ Names & Details — Is every proper noun spelled correctly? Every name right?
✓ Dates & Math — Do the numbers add up? Are deadlines accurate?
✓ Policy References — Does the cited policy actually say what the AI implies?
✓ Cultural Fit — Would this sound natural in YOUR workplace to THIS person?
✓ Self-Critique QA — Ask the AI: "Flag anything you assumed or invented."
5 checks. 60 seconds. Catches 90% of AI writing errors before they reach your colleagues.
This is the practical takeaway. Five checks that take 60 seconds total. The Self-Critique QA prompt is powerful — asking the AI to be its own editor catches a surprising number of hallucinations. Print this as a desk card for participants.
🧠 QUICK CHECK
An AI draft says "As per our company's 90-day review policy..." but you never mentioned any such policy. Which failure pattern is this?
A Wrong Assumptions
B Tone Mismatch
C Invented Details
D Over-Polishing
Interactive quiz. Answer C is correct. Invented Details is the most dangerous pattern because fabricated policy references can create legal exposure. The AI didn't 'lie' — it pattern-matched from its training data to produce something that sounds like corporate communication. But the specific reference doesn't exist.
04
Email Clinic
Rewrite professional communications with AI assistance
Transition to first activity. The Email Clinic gives participants three pre-loaded workplace communications that need improvement. They'll use AI tools to rewrite each one, then document their decisions about which AI suggestions they accepted or rejected.

Activity: Email Clinic

📧 EMAIL 1: Internal Request
"Hey, I need the sales report by end of day. You were supposed to have it done already. Send it over ASAP."
📧 EMAIL 2: Time-off Request
"I want to take next Friday off. I have stuff to do. Let me know if that works or whatever."
📧 EMAIL 3: Complaint Response
"We got your complaint. Not sure what you expected us to do about it. The policy is the policy."
📋 Your Task (20 min)
1. Use AI to rewrite each email using CRAFT
2. Document which suggestions you accepted/rejected
3. Run the 60-second verification checklist on each
💡 Pro Tip
Set a role first: "You are an executive communication coach." Then provide the original email as context with specific improvement goals.
Hands-on activity. Direct participants to the exercise platform on their devices. Walk the room while they work. The key learning: participants will see how different prompting approaches produce dramatically different rewrites. The decisions log is crucial — it builds the habit of critical evaluation rather than blind acceptance.
05
Template Builder
Create reusable prompt templates for your daily work
Transition to Template Builder activity. This is where participants create a reusable prompt template they can use repeatedly for common work tasks. The template becomes a personal productivity asset they take away from the workshop.

Activity: Template Builder

🧱 Template Structure
1. Role: Who the AI should be
2. Goal: The specific outcome
3. Details: Context & requirements
4. Rules: Constraints & limits
5. QA Prompt: Self-check instruction
💡 Example Template
Role: Senior HR advisor at a mid-size firm
Goal: Draft a performance feedback email
Details: Employee exceeded Q3 targets by 20%...
Rules: Max 150 words. No invented metrics.
QA: "Flag any details not provided by me."
📋 Your Task (15 min)
Build a reusable template for a task YOU do repeatedly (weekly reports, client updates, meeting summaries). Fill in all 5 fields. Test it with your AI tool — does it produce consistent, high-quality output?
Hands-on activity. The Template Builder is about creating lasting productivity gains — not one-off prompts, but reusable templates for recurring tasks. Direct participants to think about their most common writing tasks. Walk the room and help anyone stuck. A good template should produce 80% usable output every time.
06
Peer Review & Writing Clinic
Give structured feedback, then apply skills in realistic scenarios
Transition to peer activities. Peer Review builds the skill of evaluating AI-assisted writing using the Seven Cs framework. The Writing Clinic then applies everything learned to realistic workplace scenarios. These activities reinforce critical evaluation skills.

Activities: Peer Review & Writing Clinic

🔄 Peer Review Swap (15 min)
Review a colleague's Email Clinic draft using the Seven Cs Framework:
C lear C oncise C oncrete C orrect
C oherent C omplete C ourteous
📝 Writing Clinic (20 min)
Choose 2 scenarios and draft AI-assisted responses:
• Diplomatic Feedback to a colleague
• Angry Client Response
• Team Change Announcement
• Repurposing Content for newsletter
• Job Application / Cover Letter
Rate each C from 1-5 with specific comments. For Writing Clinic, use CRAFT + constraints + verification checklist.
Two activities in sequence. Peer Review: participants swap Email Clinic drafts and provide structured feedback using the Seven Cs. This builds evaluation skills. Writing Clinic: participants choose 2 of 5 realistic scenarios and apply everything they've learned. Walk the room, encourage CRAFT usage, and remind about the verification checklist.
🧠 QUICK CHECK
An AI-generated email says "we'll get back to you soon" without specifying when. Which of the Seven Cs does this violate?
A Clear — it's ambiguous
B Concrete — it lacks specifics
C Courteous — it's dismissive
D Correct — it's factually wrong
Interactive quiz. Answer B is correct. 'Soon' is vague — it violates Concrete. The reader can't act on it. AI frequently uses vague language like 'soon,' 'shortly,' 'in due course' because these are common patterns in its training data. A human editor adds specifics.

Key Takeaways

🧠  LLMs predict tokens. Your prompt sets the prediction context. Clearer input → stronger patterns → better output.
🎯  Use CRAFT. Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone — at least 3 elements in every prompt you write.
🛡️  Add constraints. What to change, what to keep, what to avoid. Always include the anchor guardrail.
✅  Verify everything. 60-second checklist: names, dates, policies, cultural fit, self-critique QA.
