## PRESS RELEASE

**Synaptic Gives AI Consultants One Platform to Run Their Entire Practice — Under Their Own Brand**

*From client intake to post-training report, Synaptic replaces the disconnected tools that slow consultants down and puts everything in one place.*

June 11, 2026 — Today, Synaptic launches as the first end-to-end operating platform built specifically for independent AI consultants and training professionals. Synaptic consolidates client intake, proposal building, content management, live session delivery, and post-training reporting into a single white-labeled workspace — so consultants spend their time delivering value to clients, not managing a stack of disconnected tools.

Before Synaptic, an AI consultant running three concurrent training engagements might use five or more tools in a single workday: a form builder to capture client inquiries, a word processor to write proposals, a cloud drive to store and share training materials, a live polling tool to run in-session activities, a spreadsheet to track revenue, and a presentation tool to compile a post-training report. None of these tools talk to each other. A proposal built in one tool has no connection to the training content assembled in another, which has no connection to the quiz results captured in a third. Every post-training summary requires the consultant to manually gather data from multiple sources and assemble it by hand — a process that can take hours per engagement.

Synaptic changes this. When a prospective client fills out a training request form — branded with the consultant's own logo and colors — the submission lands directly in a structured inbox where the consultant reviews, accepts, and converts it into a client profile and program record in seconds. From there, the consultant builds a proposal from a configured catalogue of their services, adjusts pricing, attaches terms, and exports a professional PDF — all without leaving the platform. Once the proposal is accepted, the consultant assembles a tailored content package from their library, generates a session access code for participants, and runs the training with built-in live quizzes, polls, and feedback collection. When the program ends, Synaptic auto-generates a branded post-training summary report — complete with assessment results, learning comparisons, and participant feedback trends — ready to share with the client.

"I used to spend the last two hours of every training engagement pulling together a summary report from three different tools," said TK, an AI training consultant based in TK. "With Synaptic, I generate the post-training summary in one click. It has my logo, my branding, and all the data already in it. My clients think I have a full operations team behind me."

Synaptic is built on a white-label foundation. Clients and participants interact exclusively with the consultant's brand — the platform's own name never appears on any client-facing surface. Participant sign-up pages, assessment forms, proposal PDFs, and email notifications all carry the consultant's logo, colors, and business name. The consultant's workspace lives at a custom subdomain (e.g., yourname.synaptic.app), and all outgoing communications are sent from the consultant's configured email address.

"When my client fills out the intake form or receives a proposal from me, it looks exactly like it came from my consulting practice," said TK, a digital transformation consultant in TK. "Synaptic is running in the background, but my clients only ever see my brand."

Synaptic also brings structure to the content that sits at the heart of every training engagement. Consultants can build a library of slide decks, facilitator guides, exercises, assessments, YouTube videos, and internet resources — all tagged by audience level, delivery mode, and engagement type. When preparing for a new session, the consultant filters the library by context, assembles a session-specific content package, and adds delivery notes alongside each item. The built-in question bank stores assessments and quizzes that can be mixed and matched across sessions — deployed as pre-session profiling tools, live comprehension checks, or post-training evaluations — with results automatically captured and tied to the session record.

Synaptic is available today for AI consultants and training professionals. The platform is designed for a single consultant workspace in its first version, with multi-consultant and team workspaces planned for a future release.

## FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

---

### FAQ 1: What is the problem Synaptic solves?

Independent AI consultants and training professionals manage complex, multi-stage client engagements using a collection of unconnected tools. A single engagement — from the first client inquiry to the final post-training report — can require a form builder, word processor, cloud drive, presentation software, a polling tool, a spreadsheet, and an email client. None of these tools share data. The result is hours of manual work per engagement: retyping client information, manually compiling assessment results, rebuilding proposals from scratch for each new client, and assembling post-training reports by hand.

The problem compounds as a consultant's practice grows. Three concurrent clients might mean three separate folder structures, three sets of manually tracked invoice statuses, three individually compiled assessment summaries — all stored in different places, with no unified view of what is active, what is due, and what has been delivered. A TK-hour training program generates TK hours of administrative overhead that Synaptic eliminates.

There is also a professionalism gap. The tools consultants cobble together were not designed for client-facing use. A Google Form for client intake, a Dropbox link for proposals, and a Kahoot game code for assessments create a fragmented, inconsistent experience for clients — one that does not reflect the quality of the consultant's actual delivery.

Synaptic consolidates the entire engagement lifecycle into one platform and wraps it in the consultant's own brand.

They typically manage two to ten concurrent client engagements, deliver a mix of online and in-person sessions, and operate as sole practitioners or within small consulting practices.

Secondary customers in V2 are small and mid-size consulting firms that want to standardize how their team of consultants manages client engagements, content delivery, and reporting on a single shared platform.

### FAQ 3: What does Synaptic do?

Synaptic gives consultants one workspace to run their entire practice across four connected modules:

**Client Onboarding and Proposal Management.** Clients submit training requests through a white-labeled registration form. The consultant reviews requests in a structured inbox, accepts or declines them, and converts accepted requests into client profiles. From there, the consultant builds a proposal by selecting from a configured service offerings catalogue, adjusting costs and timelines, attaching terms and conditions, and exporting a branded PDF. A Pipeline Kanban view shows every engagement from first inquiry to active program.

**Content Library.** A central repository for all training and consulting materials — slide decks, facilitator guides, exercises, videos, external links, and assessments. The consultant tags content by audience level, delivery mode, and service type, then assembles session-specific content packages. A built-in question bank powers pre-session profiling assessments, live quizzes, polls, end-of-day feedback forms, and post-training evaluations — all deployed to participants through time-limited, code-based access pages.

**Session Management.** Sessions are created, scheduled, and managed through a structured lifecycle from Draft through Archived. Each session carries a timeline, milestones, a pre-flight checklist, a content package, and a participant roster. A calendar view shows all sessions across all clients, with conflict detection and ICS export. Automated notifications alert the consultant to upcoming sessions, overdue milestones, and required status transitions.

**Dashboard and Reporting.** The main dashboard aggregates the most critical signals from all three modules — active sessions, upcoming sessions, pending client requests, overdue milestones, pipeline value, and outstanding invoices — in a single at-a-glance view. The reporting suite covers engagement pipeline analytics, session delivery metrics, assessment and learning analytics (including pre/post learning comparison), content performance, and revenue summaries. At the end of every program, Synaptic generates a branded post-training summary PDF — ready to share with the client.

### FAQ 6: What are the biggest risks and how will Synaptic mitigate them?

**Risk 1: Over-engineering V1 for a single user.**
The platform is being built by one user for one user in V1. The risk is that requirements reflect personal preferences rather than generalized consultant needs. Mitigation: every requirement is framed as a user story with acceptance criteria that would apply to any AI consultant, not just the founding user. The architecture is explicitly designed for multi-tenancy from day one.

**Risk 2: Participant adoption of the code-based sign-in.**
Participants join sessions via a name and access code — no account required. If participants find this flow confusing or lose the code, session delivery is disrupted. Mitigation: the sign-up page is mobile-optimized, the code is short and alphanumeric, the consultant has a one-click code regeneration option, and the validity window prevents expired-code confusion.

**Risk 3: Content library becomes a cluttered dumping ground.**
Without discipline, the Content Library grows into an unmanageable collection of outdated or duplicate files. Mitigation: mandatory tagging on upload, content versioning, usage tracking (items that have never been used in a session are surfaced), and archive functionality.

**Risk 4: White-label quality falls short of professional standards.**
If the branded PDFs, forms, and pages look generic or misaligned with the consultant's visual identity, the white-label value proposition fails. Mitigation: a live branding preview mode lets the consultant see exactly how their logo and colors render on every surface before publishing. PDF templates are professionally designed.

**Risk 5: Data lock-in concern from future multi-tenant consultants.**
Consultants evaluating Synaptic in V2 will want assurance that their client data, content, and reports are portable. Mitigation: full data export (CSV and PDF) is a P1 feature. Full workspace data export is a V2 requirement prior to launch.

### FAQ 7: How could Synaptic go bigger?

The V1 platform is a single-consultant workspace. The V2 vision is a platform where any independent AI consultant or consulting firm can sign up for their own Synaptic workspace — instantly provisioned, fully branded, and ready to run a complete engagement in hours.

Beyond multi-tenancy, the long-term vision includes:
- **AI-powered session intelligence:** the platform analyzes assessment results, feedback trends, and content usage patterns across all sessions to recommend adjustments — suggesting which content performs best for which audience profile, or flagging when a participant group is tracking below expected comprehension benchmarks.
- **Client portal:** a read-only, branded client portal where clients can track program progress, view milestones, and access shared reports — without logging in to the consultant's workspace.
- **Marketplace for content:** consultants can optionally share or sell content packages (assessment question banks, training modules) with other Synaptic consultants through a curated marketplace.
- **Credentialing and certification:** Synaptic-issued certificates of completion for participants, branded to the consultant's practice, generated automatically at program close.

### FAQ 8: What are the dogs not barking?

**No signal yet on peer benchmarking appetite.** Synaptic's V2 roadmap includes cross-consultant benchmarking — comparing assessment scores, feedback ratings, and proposal acceptance rates across the platform. There is no expressed demand for this feature from the founding user. It is a platform-level assumption, not a validated need. It may be that consultants are protective of their performance data and unwilling to share it, even anonymously.

**No client feedback on the white-label experience.** The assumption that clients will notice and value a consistent, branded experience is based on the consultant's hypothesis, not direct client feedback. It is possible that clients are indifferent to whether a form comes from "your consulting practice" or "a platform you use." This assumption should be tested with two or three clients before investing heavily in white-label surface refinement.

**No signal on content sharing between sessions of the same client.** The content library is designed as a global repository reused across clients. It is unclear whether consultants will actually want to share the same content items across different client engagements, or whether each engagement will feel custom enough that they rebuild content from scratch anyway.

### FAQ 9: What are the hotly debated decisions?

**Should the participant experience be richer in V1?**

*The dilemma:* Currently, participants interact with Synaptic only through the session access code sign-up page, assessment forms, and feedback submissions. There is no participant-facing view of session materials, schedules, or outcomes. The consultant controls everything. However, participants in a multi-day training might benefit from being able to see their own assessment results, access shared resources, or complete pre-work before they arrive.

*The case for keeping it minimal:* A participant-facing portal adds significant design, development, and privacy complexity. In V1, with a single consultant user, there is no validated demand for this feature. The consultant is the customer; participants are end recipients of the consultant's delivery.

*The case for expanding it:* Participants who can access their own results, review session materials, and see their progress are more engaged learners. A participant portal would differentiate Synaptic from simpler assessment tools and create a more complete learning experience.

*Current plan:* Minimal participant experience in V1. Full participant portal assessed for V2 based on consultant feedback after initial deployments.

### FAQ 11: What alternatives were considered?

**Build on top of an existing LMS (e.g., Moodle, Canvas, Teachable).**
Existing LMS platforms are designed for asynchronous, course-based learning. They lack proposal management, client intake, milestone tracking, and a white-label commercial layer. Adapting an LMS to support the consulting engagement lifecycle would require more customization than building a purpose-built tool.

**Use a CRM (e.g., HubSpot, Salesforce) as the backbone.**
CRMs handle the commercial pipeline well but have no native content library, assessment delivery, live quiz capability, or post-training reporting. The integration overhead to connect a CRM with assessment tools, calendar tools, and PDF generators would recreate the fragmentation problem Synaptic is designed to solve.

**Stick with the existing multi-tool stack.**
The status quo — using separate tools for each function — is the baseline. It works, but at a cost of hours per engagement in administrative overhead and at the cost of a fragmented, inconsistent client experience. As the number of concurrent engagements grows, the status quo does not scale.

**Build a simple client portal only.**
A lightweight client-facing portal (intake form + proposal) addresses the first two steps of the engagement but leaves content management, live assessment delivery, session tracking, and reporting unsolved. It solves a cosmetic problem without addressing the operational one.
