From Tokens to Output
How LLMs actually process your prompts — the five-step pipeline
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Before we learn HOW to write great prompts, let's understand what happens to them inside the model."] Key talking points: • This builds directly on Module 1's autocomplete model • Understanding the pipeline explains WHY prompt techniques work Facilitation tip: "If you know why specific prompts work better, you'll never need to memorise rules — you'll derive them."

The Five-Step Pipeline

1. TOKENIZE
✂️
Your text is split into tokensTokens are sub-word pieces. "Prompt engineering" = 2 tokens. Models process tokens, not words — this is why token limits matter. (sub-word pieces)
→
2. EMBED
🧮
Each token becomes a high-dimensional number vector
→
3. ATTEND
🔍
AttentionAttention lets the model weigh which parts of your prompt matter most for each word it generates. Specific prompts create stronger attention signals. weighs which tokens relate to each other
→
4. PREDICT
🎯
Model calculates probability for every possible next token
→
5. SAMPLE
🎲
One token is chosen based on temperature setting
💡 This repeats for EVERY token — a 500-word response runs this pipeline ~700 times
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Your prompt goes on a journey before you see any output. Here's what that journey looks like."] Key talking points: • Walk through each step — tokenize (Module 1 recap), embed (numbers!), attend (relationships), predict (probabilities), sample (pick one) • The ATTEND step is key: this is where your prompt structure matters most • More specific prompts create stronger attention signals → better predictions • This is NOT a database lookup — it's computed fresh every time Discussion prompt: "Given this pipeline, why do you think longer, more specific prompts produce better output?" Facilitation tip: Use the pipeline to preview WHY CRAFT works. "In Chapter 2, you'll see how each CRAFT element optimises a different step in this pipeline."

The CRAFT Framework
Five elements that transform vague requests into precision instruments
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Now you know WHY specific prompts work. Let's learn a repeatable system for writing them."] Key talking points: • CRAFT is our framework — not the only one, but a solid professional system • Each letter maps to a different dimension of prompt quality Facilitation tip: Build anticipation — "By end of this chapter you'll have a 5-element checklist you can use for ANY prompt."

The CRAFT Framework

C
Context
Background info the AI needs to understand your situation
R
Role
Who you want the AI to "be" — the expert persona
A
Action
The specific task — what you want it to DO
F
Format
How the output should be structured
T
Tone
Voice, formality level, and style
💡 You don't always need all 5 — but the more you include, the more precise the output
🔗 Pipeline connection: Context & Role shape Embedding → Action drives Attention → Format & Tone narrow Sampling
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's the framework that ties everything together — CRAFT."] Key talking points: • Walk through each letter with a quick example • C: "I'm a project manager at a tech company preparing for a board meeting" • R: "Act as a senior communications consultant" • A: "Write an executive summary of Q3 progress" • F: "3 paragraphs, bullet points for metrics, max 200 words" • T: "Professional, confident, data-driven" Discussion prompt: "Which element do you think most people skip? Why?" Facilitation tip: Most skip Context and Format. Highlight that these two give the biggest quality jump for least effort.

Weak vs Strong: Same Task, Different Results

❌ WEAK PROMPT
"Write me an email about the project delay"
Missing: Context, Role, Format, Tone. AI will guess everything — and probably guess wrong.
→
✅ CRAFT PROMPT
C: Our Q3 product launch is delayed 2 weeks due to vendor issues.
R: Act as a senior project manager.
A: Write a stakeholder update email explaining the delay and revised timeline.
F: 3 paragraphs: situation, impact, next steps. Under 150 words.
T: Professional, transparent, solutions-focused.
📊 In testing, CRAFT prompts produce usable output on first try ~80% of the time vs ~20% for vague prompts
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Let me show you the difference CRAFT makes with a concrete example."] Key talking points: • Read the weak prompt aloud — ask "what would YOU write if given only this?" • The AI faces the same ambiguity problem — it doesn't know your company, audience, or tone preference • Walk through the CRAFT version — show how each element removes a guess • 80% first-try success rate is based on professional workflow testing Discussion prompt: "What would the AI guess wrong with just the weak prompt?" Facilitation tip: Have someone volunteer what they think the AI would assume with the weak version. Usually: wrong tone, wrong length, wrong audience.

03
Constraints — Your Safety Net
Tell AI what NOT to do — and watch the quality jump
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "CRAFT tells AI what to do. Constraints tell it what NOT to do. Both matter."] Key talking points: • Constraints are the guardrails that prevent AI from going off track • Think of them as the bumpers in bowling — they keep the ball in the lane Facilitation tip: "If CRAFT is the steering wheel, constraints are the lane markings."

Three Types of Constraints

✅
What to CHANGE
"Rewrite this in active voice"
"Convert bullet points to paragraphs"
"Shorten each section to 2 sentences"
🔒
What to KEEP
"Keep all original numbers and dates"
"Preserve the 3-section structure"
"Maintain the existing tone"
🚫
What to AVOID
"Do not use jargon or acronyms"
"Never invent statistics or sources"
"Avoid passive voice entirely"
💡 Pro tip: The AVOID column prevents the most costly AI mistakes — always include at least one
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "There are three flavours of constraint — each does a different job."] Key talking points: • CHANGE: tells AI what transformation to apply • KEEP: protects specific elements from being altered (critical for editing tasks!) • AVOID: prevents common AI failure patterns • The AVOID column is the most powerful for professional work Discussion prompt: "What's one thing AI has done in your writing that you wish it hadn't?" Facilitation tip: Collect 3-4 answers — then show how each maps to an AVOID constraint.

The Anchor Guardrail

"Do not invent any details, statistics, or claims that I have not explicitly provided in this prompt."
Why it works
LLMs hallucinateHallucination = when AI generates confident-sounding information that isn't true. It's a fundamental feature of probability-based generation, not a bug that can be fully fixed. by default — they fill gaps with plausible-sounding content. This constraint forces the model to stay within your provided facts.
When to use it
• Emails that reference specific projects or deadlines
• Reports with data or metrics
• Any content going to external audiences
• Anything with legal or compliance implications
🛡️ Add this to every professional prompt. It's the single most impactful constraint for workplace writing.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Of all possible constraints, this one earns its own slide."] Key talking points: • This is the #1 constraint for professional use — memorise it • Without it, AI will confidently invent policy names, statistics, dates, and quotes • It doesn't guarantee zero hallucination but dramatically reduces it • Think of it as the "safety catch" on a weapon — always on by default Discussion prompt: "Has anyone received AI output that included invented details? What happened?" Facilitation tip: Share a specific example — "I asked for a policy summary and it invented a 'Remote Work Policy 2024' that didn't exist."

The Art of Iteration
Progressive prompting — why your second prompt matters more than your first
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "CRAFT + Constraints get you 80% there on the first try. Iteration gets you to 95%."] Key talking points: • Professional prompt engineers almost never accept first-draft output • The skill is knowing HOW to iterate — not just "try again" Facilitation tip: "Your first prompt is the rough sketch. Iteration is the refinement. Both are part of the craft."

The 3-Step Iteration Workflow

1. STRUCTURE
🏗️
First prompt: get the bones right. Focus on CRAFT + overall shape of the output.
→
2. REFINE
✨
Second prompt: fix specifics. Adjust tone, length, emphasis, or missing details.
→
3. VERIFY
✅
Third prompt: challenge it. "What did you assume? What might be wrong?"
💡 Most professionals stop at step 1. Step 3 is what separates good output from great output.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's the 3-step pattern that professional prompt engineers use."] Key talking points: • Step 1 is CRAFT + Constraints — what they already know • Step 2 is targeted refinement — "make paragraph 2 shorter" or "add more data in section 3" • Step 3 is the secret weapon — asking the AI to critique its own work • Show that this is faster than writing from scratch — 3 prompts take 2-3 minutes total Discussion prompt: "Who currently stops at step 1? That's normal — but let's change that." Facilitation tip: Emphasise that iteration is NOT failure — it's the intended workflow.

Iteration in Action: Email Task

PROMPT 1 — STRUCTURE
"You are a project manager. Write a team email announcing our office move from Building A to Building C, effective March 15. Include logistics, timeline, and what people need to do. 3 sections, professional tone, under 200 words. Do not invent details I haven't provided."
PROMPT 2 — REFINE
"Good structure. Now: make the tone warmer and more encouraging. Add a bullet list for the 'what to do' section. Mention that IT will handle equipment moves — people only need to pack personal items by March 12."
PROMPT 3 — VERIFY
"Review this email: Are there any assumptions you made that I didn't confirm? Is anything potentially unclear to a reader? Suggest 2 improvements."
⏱️ Total time: ~3 minutes for a polished, verified email you'd actually send
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Let me walk through a real 3-prompt iteration so you can see the pattern."] Key talking points: • Prompt 1: full CRAFT — gets the structure and overall content • Prompt 2: targeted refinements — doesn't rewrite, just adjusts • Prompt 3: self-critique — the AI flags its own assumptions and blind spots • Total time: 3 prompts, 3 minutes, professional-quality output • Key insight: each prompt is SHORT — you're steering, not rewriting Discussion prompt: "Which step surprised you most? Did you expect asking AI to critique itself would work?" Facilitation tip: If time allows, show this live — run the 3 prompts and show the evolution in real-time.

When AI Gets It Wrong
Four failure patterns every professional must recognise
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "You now have tools to write great prompts. But what about catching bad output?"] Key talking points: • Even great prompts can produce flawed output — you need to know what to look for • These 4 patterns account for ~90% of AI writing failures in professional contexts Facilitation tip: "This chapter is your quality control training. You'll use it every single time you use AI for work."

The Four Failure Patterns

🎭
Wrong Assumptions
AI assumes context you never provided — guesses your company size, industry, or situation incorrectly.
🎵
Tone Mismatch
Output sounds like a corporate robot or an overly casual friend — neither matches YOUR voice.
🌀
Invented Details
AI fabricates specific data, policy names, dates, or quotes that sound real but aren't.
💎
Over-Polishing
Output is technically correct but sounds nothing like you — too formal, too perfect, clearly AI-written.
🛡️ Prevention: Context fixes #1, Tone in CRAFT fixes #2, Anchor Guardrail fixes #3, Style examples fix #4
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Even with perfect prompts, AI can fail in predictable ways. Here are the four you'll see most."] Key talking points: • Wrong Assumptions: caused by missing Context in your prompt • Tone Mismatch: caused by missing or vague Tone specification • Invented Details: the hallucination problem — Anchor Guardrail is your fix • Over-Polishing: the "AI voice" problem — fix by providing examples of YOUR writing style • Each failure maps to a CRAFT element or constraint they already know Discussion prompt: "Which of these have you experienced? Which worries you most?" Facilitation tip: Show a real example of each — even 1 sentence per pattern makes it concrete.

The 60-Second Verification Checklist

Run this on EVERY AI draft before sending. Takes under 60 seconds.

1️⃣ Facts check — Are all dates, names, numbers, and claims actually true?
2️⃣ Tone check — Does this sound like ME or like a generic AI?
3️⃣ Audience check — Would the recipient find this appropriate and clear?
4️⃣ Completeness check — Is anything important missing or oversimplified?
5️⃣ Risk check — Could anything here cause harm if it's wrong?
⏱️ This catches 90% of AI errors. Make it a habit — never send AI output without these 5 checks.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's your quality control process — memorise these 5 checks."] Key talking points: • Walk through each check with a quick example • Facts: did it make up a "company policy" that doesn't exist? • Tone: does it sound robotic? Did it add "I hope this email finds you well"? • Audience: would your VP find this appropriate? Would your direct report? • Completeness: did it skip something because you forgot to mention it? • Risk: if this went out wrong, what's the worst case? Discussion prompt: "Which check do you think catches the most errors in practice?" Facilitation tip: Answer is usually #1 (Facts) — AI is confidently wrong more often than people expect.

Building Your Prompt Library

Reusable templates with [VARIABLES]Variables are placeholders in your template that you swap out each time you use it. Like a form with blanks — the structure stays the same, only the specifics change. you swap out each time — write once, use forever.

TEMPLATE EXAMPLE: Stakeholder Update
Context: I am [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. [PROJECT_NAME] is currently [STATUS].
Role: Act as a senior project communicator.
Action: Write a stakeholder update email covering [TOPIC].
Format: 3 sections: Progress, Risks, Next Steps. Bullet points. Under [WORD_LIMIT] words.
Tone: [TONE_STYLE] — professional and [TONE_MODIFIER].
Constraints: Do not invent details. Keep all dates accurate. Avoid jargon.
Why templates work
You invest effort ONCE to get the structure right. Then reuse it 50+ times by just swapping variables.
What to templatise
Weekly updates, meeting summaries, feedback emails, project proposals, status reports, onboarding messages.

Key Takeaways

🎯 CRAFT is your foundation
Context, Role, Action, Format, Tone — use all 5 for any important prompt. Each element removes one guess the AI would otherwise make.
🛡️ Always constrain
The Anchor Guardrail ("don't invent details") belongs in every professional prompt. Add CHANGE, KEEP, and AVOID constraints to narrow output.
🔄 Iterate, don't restart
Structure → Refine → Verify. Three short prompts beat one long one. Iteration is the skill, not a sign of failure.
✅ Always verify
60-second checklist: Facts, Tone, Audience, Completeness, Risk. Never send AI output without human review.
💡 Just use CRAFT + Anchor Guardrail + Iterate. That covers 90% of cases.
