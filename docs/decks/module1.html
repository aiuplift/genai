How Generative AI Works
Tokens, prediction, and the illusion of understanding
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Now that you know where we're headed, let's start with the big question — how does this actually work?"] Key talking points: • This chapter demystifies the "magic" — it's math, not intelligence • We'll see exactly how AI processes text Facilitation tip: Build curiosity here. "By end of this section you'll understand why AI sometimes says confidently wrong things."

The World's Most Powerful Autocomplete

1. TRAINING
📚
Reads billions of pages of text from the internet, books, and code
→
2. PATTERNS
🧩
Learns statistical patterns: which words tend to follow which other words
→
3. OUTPUT
✍️
Predicts the next tokenA token is a piece of text — roughly ¾ of a word. "ChatGPT" = 3 tokens. "Hello world" = 2 tokens. AI thinks in tokens, not words., one at a time, to form responses
💡 Key insight: GenAI doesn't "know" things — it predicts what text is most likely to come next
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Let's start with what's actually happening under the hood"] Key talking points: • This is the single most important mental model for the course • AI = sophisticated pattern matching, NOT understanding • The training data determines what patterns it learned • "Autocomplete on steroids" — same idea as phone keyboard, just way more powerful Discussion prompt: "Your phone predicts your next word too. What's the difference in scale?" Facilitation tip: Use the typing animation metaphor — show phone autocomplete, then explain AI is the same principle at massive scale.

Pattern Matching ≠ Understanding

✅ What AI DOES
• Recognise language patterns
• Generate fluent text
• Translate between languages
• Summarise long documents
• Follow formatting instructions
• Combine ideas from training data
❌ What AI DOESN'T
• Understand meaning or truth
• Have beliefs or opinions
• Access real-time information
• Verify facts before stating them
• Know what it doesn't know
• Think or reason like humans
💡 AI is a mirror of its training data — it reflects patterns, not truth
[TIMING: 2 min] [TRANSITION: "So now we know HOW it works. But what does that mean for what it CAN and CAN'T do?"] Key talking points: • This is the most misunderstood aspect of AI • "Fluent" does not mean "correct" — AI can write beautifully wrong answers • No real-time info: training data has a cutoff date • It doesn't know what it doesn't know — it can't say "I'm not sure" Discussion prompt: "Have you ever been confidently told something wrong by ChatGPT?" Facilitation tip: Collect 2-3 examples from the room. This naturally leads to the next slide on hallucination.

The HallucinationWhen AI generates text that sounds confident and plausible but is factually incorrect. It's not "lying" — it simply predicted likely-sounding tokens without any truth-checking mechanism. Problem

"AI hallucination isn't a bug — it's a fundamental feature of how prediction works. The model doesn't distinguish between what's true and what sounds true."
— Why this matters for every professional
📚 Fake Citations
Lawyers fined for citing AI-generated case law that didn't exist (2023)
📊 Wrong Numbers
Financial reports with plausible but fabricated statistics that passed initial review
👤 Invented People
AI creates realistic biographies for people who never existed, complete with quotes
🛡️ Rule #1: Never trust AI output without verification — especially for facts, numbers, and names
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "And this leads to the biggest risk you need to understand RIGHT NOW"] Key talking points: • Hallucination = AI "makes up" information that sounds plausible • Not lying — it's predicting tokens without truth-checking • Real-world consequences: legal, financial, reputational • This is why we need verification habits (covered in Chapter 4) Discussion prompt: "What would happen in YOUR work if AI gave you a convincing but wrong answer?" Facilitation tip: The lawyer example is powerful — mention it was a $5,000 fine and nearly ended careers. Make it real.

Four Risks Every AI User Must Know

🎭
HallucinationAI generates confident-sounding but factually wrong information. It cannot distinguish fact from fiction — it only predicts likely-sounding text.
AI states false information with full confidence. No "I'm not sure" — just wrong answers that sound right.
🔒
Privacy & Data LeakageAnything you paste into an AI tool may be used for training or stored on external servers. Company secrets, personal data, and confidential info can leak.
Data you share with AI may be stored, used for training, or visible to the provider. Company secrets at risk.
⚖️
Bias & FairnessAI models inherit biases from their training data. If the training data over-represents certain perspectives, the AI will too. This can perpetuate stereotypes and unfair outcomes.
Training data biases become output biases. Gender, racial, and cultural stereotypes can be amplified.
©️
Copyright & IPAI may generate content similar to copyrighted material. Legal ownership of AI-generated content is still evolving. Using AI output without review can create legal liability.
Who owns AI-generated content? Can AI accidentally reproduce copyrighted work? Legal landscape still evolving.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Every tool has risks. AI's risks are unique because the output LOOKS trustworthy."] Key talking points: • Walk through each risk with a 1-sentence real-world example • Hallucination: lawyer case. Privacy: Samsung code leak. Bias: hiring tools. Copyright: Getty vs Stability AI • These aren't edge cases — they happen daily to professionals Discussion prompt: "Which of these four risks is most relevant to YOUR work?" Facilitation tip: Don't dwell too long on each — the next slide gives them actionable safety rules.

Your AI Safety Checklist

✓ Verify before you trust. Always fact-check AI output — especially names, numbers, and citations.
✓ Never paste confidential data. Treat every AI tool as a public billboard unless your org has approved it.
✓ Check for bias. Ask: "Would this output be different if the person's name/gender/background changed?"
✓ Own the output. If you use AI-generated content, YOU are responsible — not the AI.
✓ Stay current. AI policies and capabilities change monthly. Review your company's AI policy regularly.
💡 The Golden Rule
If you wouldn't publish it without checking, don't trust AI to get it right either. Human judgment remains the final filter.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "So what do we DO about these risks? Here are 5 non-negotiable rules."] Key talking points: • These are the minimum safety standards for professional AI use • Rule 2 is the one most people violate unknowingly — give examples of what counts as "confidential" • Rule 4 is critical: "The AI made a mistake" is never an acceptable excuse in professional settings • The golden rule: AI output needs the same scrutiny as any other source Discussion prompt: "Has your organisation published an AI use policy? Do you know what it says?" Facilitation tip: If they don't know their company's AI policy, that becomes a homework assignment.

The 30-Second Verification Check

⏱️ Take 2 minutes — apply this to any recent AI output you've received
❓
Question 1
"Can I verify this claim with a second source?"
🔍
Question 2
"Would I stake my reputation on this being correct?"
⚠️
Question 3
"What's the worst that happens if this is wrong?"
  • 1 Think of the last time you used AI for work (or think of a hypothetical)
  • 2 Apply all 3 questions to that output
  • 3 Decide: Would you have published it as-is?
If ANY answer is "no" or "not sure" → verify before using. 30 seconds of checking saves hours of correction.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Let me give you a quick mental framework you can use every single time"] Key talking points: • This is a habit to build — not a one-time exercise • Question 3 is the risk assessment: low stakes = trust more freely; high stakes = verify everything • Real professionals already do this for other sources (Wikipedia, colleague advice) — apply same standard to AI Discussion prompt: "Share with your neighbour: what was your answer to Question 3?" Facilitation tip: Give them 90 seconds of quiet thinking, then 30 seconds of sharing. Keep it tight.
