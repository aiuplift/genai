02
The Resolution Ladder
One document, three summaries — each serving a different audience
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Let's make this practical."] Key talking points: • This chapter teaches the core technique: multi-resolution summarisation • Think of it like a camera zoom — same scene, different levels of detail • One-line (elevator pitch), paragraph (executive brief), full page (detailed summary) • Each resolution serves a different purpose and audience

Three Levels of Summary

ONE-LINE
Elevator Pitch
The single most important takeaway. Use for: subject lines, Slack updates, opening statements.
→
PARAGRAPH
Executive Brief
3-5 sentences covering key points. Use for: email summaries, handouts, meeting prep notes.
→
FULL PAGE
Detailed Summary
Structured overview with all key details. Use for: briefing documents, handover notes, reference summaries.
🎯 Each resolution serves a DIFFERENT audience — the skill is choosing which level to deliver
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's the framework."] Key talking points: • One-Line: forces AI (and you) to identify THE single most important point. Hardest to write, most valuable. • Paragraph: the "executive summary" level — enough context for someone to make a decision without reading the full doc. • Full Page: comprehensive but structured — someone could skip the original doc entirely (with verification caveats). • The key skill isn't asking AI to summarise — it's knowing which resolution your audience needs. • Example: "Your CEO needs the one-liner. Your manager needs the paragraph. The project team needs the page." Discussion prompt: "When was the last time you received a summary that was the wrong length? Too short? Too long?"

03
Extracting Themes
Beyond summarising — pull specific types of information from any document
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Summarising compresses everything equally. But sometimes you need to pull specific things OUT."] Key talking points: • Summarisation compresses the whole document • Extraction is different — it pulls out specific TYPES of information • Think of it like different lenses on the same document: themes, risks, questions, decisions, action items • This chapter teaches you to apply those lenses systematically

Beyond Summarising — 6 Extraction Lenses

🎯
Key Themes
Main topics and arguments
⚠️
Risks
Potential problems mentioned or implied
❓
Open Questions
Unanswered or unresolved issues
✅
Action Items
Tasks, commitments, next steps
🔑
Key Decisions
Choices made or required
💭
Sentiment
Tone, confidence levels, concerns
🎯 Apply any lens to any document — "Extract all [risks/questions/decisions] from this document"
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Summarisation compresses. Extraction PULLS."] Key talking points: • These 6 lenses work on ANY document type — reports, contracts, proposals, meeting minutes, emails • You don't need all 6 every time. Pick the lenses that matter for YOUR purpose. • Example prompt: "Read this proposal and extract: (1) all risks mentioned or implied, (2) any open questions that aren't answered" • AI is surprisingly good at this because extraction is pattern matching — exactly what AI does well • Caveat: AI can miss implied/subtle information. It catches explicit things well, implicit things less so. Discussion prompt: "Which of these 6 lenses would be most valuable for the documents YOU read at work?"

04
AI-Assisted Critical Review
Use AI as your "critical friend" — finding gaps, contradictions, and weak claims
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Summarisation and extraction tell you what IS in a document. Critical review tells you what's WRONG with it."] Key talking points: • This chapter shifts from passive reading to active critique • AI can review documents for: logical gaps, unsupported claims, missing context, contradictions • Think of it as having a critical colleague who reads everything carefully and asks hard questions • This is especially valuable for proposals, business cases, and draft documents

The Review Prompt Pattern

Ask AI to review a document as your "critical friend"💡 A critical friend asks the uncomfortable questions you might miss because you're too close to the content. AI has no ego investment in the document, making it effective at spotting weaknesses. — finding issues the author may have missed:
🔍 Logical Gaps — Where does the argument skip steps or assume too much?
📊 Unsupported Claims — Which statements lack evidence or citations?
🕳️ Missing Context — What important information is absent?
⚡ Contradictions — Do any parts of the document conflict with each other?
❓ Unanswered Questions — What would a skeptical reader ask after reading this?
💡 Prompt: "Review this document as a critical friend. Identify logical gaps, unsupported claims, contradictions, and questions a reader would ask."
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's how to turn AI into your critical reviewer."] Key talking points: • This is one of AI's most underused capabilities — document critique • Why it works: AI has no ego investment in the document. It's not trying to be polite. • The prompt pattern is simple: "Review this as a critical friend" + specify what you want it to look for • Important caveat: AI's critiques themselves need verification! Sometimes AI flags things that aren't actually problems. • Best practice: use this on YOUR OWN drafts before sharing with others • Real-world value: "Imagine catching a missing cost section in your proposal BEFORE the CFO reads it" Discussion prompt: "Would you rather find problems in your proposal yourself, or have your boss find them?"

05
Synthesising Multiple Sources
Find common ground, contradictions, and unique insights across documents
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "So far we've worked with single documents. Now let's tackle the hardest task: synthesising MULTIPLE sources."] Key talking points: • Synthesis is the highest-level document skill — combining insights from multiple sources • This is what researchers, analysts, and decision-makers do: read 3-5 documents and form a coherent picture • AI can help identify: common themes, contradictions, unique insights, and consensus vs disagreement • Warning: this is where AI hallucination risk is highest — it may "invent" connections

From Review to Synthesis

When you have 3-5 documents on the same topic, AI can synthesise across all of them:
🤝
Common Ground
What do all sources agree on? Where's consensus?
⚔️
Contradictions
Where do sources disagree? What are the tensions?
💎
Unique Insights
What does only ONE source mention that others miss?
⚠️ Critical Warning: AI can hallucinate consensus — claiming sources agree when they don't, or inventing connections between unrelated points. Always verify synthesis claims against each source individually.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Synthesis is the most powerful — and most dangerous — AI document skill."] Key talking points: • Synthesis = combining insights from multiple sources into a coherent picture • Three things to look for: agreement, disagreement, and unique contributions • Real-world example: "You have reports from Finance, HR, and Operations about the same initiative. What do they agree on? Where do they clash?" • CRITICAL WARNING: AI hallucination risk is highest in synthesis. AI may claim "all three sources agree that..." when actually only one does. • Verification is non-negotiable: always check synthesis claims against each original source • This is where the Anchor Guardrail matters most Discussion prompt: "Have you ever read conflicting reports about the same topic? How did you reconcile them?"

  • 3Read AI's synthesis — does it accurately represent each source?
  • 4Verify: Go back to each original excerpt. Did AI invent any agreements or contradictions?
⏱️ 6 minutes — compare AI's synthesis to your own reading of the 3 sources
