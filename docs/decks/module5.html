01
AI as Your Data Analyst
You don't need to learn formulas — you need to describe what you want
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Let's start with the fundamental shift in how non-data people can work with data."] Key talking points: • This chapter reframes who data analysis is for • Traditional approach: spend months learning Excel/SQL/Python • AI approach: describe what you need in plain English, AI writes the technical bits • This isn't about replacing data teams — it's about empowering everyone to do basic analysis

You Don't Need to Be a Data Expert

❌ THE OLD WAY
• Learn Excel formulas (weeks)
• Take a SQL course (months)
• Study Python/R for analysis (months)
• Master pivot tables and charts
• Still ask the "data person" for help
✅ THE NEW WAY
• Describe what you want in plain language
• AI generates the formula or query
• You understand what it does (AI explains)
• You verify the result manually
• You build confidence over time
🎯 The democratisation of data: AI makes analysis accessible to everyone who can describe what they need
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's the key mindset shift."] Key talking points: • The old way created a barrier: only "technical" people could work with data • The new way: if you can describe what you need, AI can help you get there • This doesn't replace data professionals — it elevates baseline capability • Example: "I want to find the average revenue by region" — you don't need to know =AVERAGEIF • Caveat: you still need to verify. AI is the bridge, not the destination. Discussion prompt: "Think of a time you needed data analysis but didn't know how to do it yourself. What would you have asked for in plain language?"

Cleaning Messy Data
Real data is never clean — AI helps you tame the chaos
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Formulas are great — when your data is clean. But real-world data almost never is."] Key talking points: • Most time spent on data analysis is actually data CLEANING (80% by most estimates) • AI doesn't fix your data automatically, but it gives you a strategy • You'll learn to identify data quality issues and describe them to AI for solutions

Three Types of Data You'll Encounter

✨
Clean Data
Consistent formats, no gaps, no duplicates. Ready to analyse immediately.
Rare in the wild 🦄
🗑️
Garbage Data
Missing values, errors, impossible numbers, corrupted entries. Needs repair.
Common from imports & legacy systems
🔀
Unorganised Data
Inconsistent formats, mixed naming, duplicates with variations. Needs standardising.
Most common in multi-person spreadsheets
💡 Most real data is a mix of garbage AND unorganised. AI helps you identify which problems you have.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Before we clean data, let's name the problems."] Key talking points: • Clean data is the unicorn — you'll rarely get it from external sources • Garbage data: missing revenue cells, dates that say "TBC", phone numbers in the email field • Unorganised data: "London", "london", "LONDON", "Ldn" — all the same city, but the spreadsheet doesn't know that • Most real datasets combine both problems • AI is excellent at identifying these patterns — it's literally pattern matching Discussion prompt: "Think of a spreadsheet you've received from a colleague or client. Which category does it fall into?"

04
From Data to Insight
Analysis, patterns, and the verification imperative
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Now your data is clean. Let's extract insights from it — carefully."] Key talking points: • Clean data is just the starting point — the value is in what it tells you • AI can spot patterns, but it can also hallucinate patterns • This chapter teaches you what AI CAN and CAN'T do with data analysis • The verification imperative: every AI insight needs a human sanity check

What AI Can See vs What It Can't

✅ AI CAN Identify
📈Patterns and trends in numbers
🔴Outliers and anomalies
🔗Correlations between columns
📊Summary statistics and groupings
🧹Data quality issues
❌ AI CANNOT Determine
⚠️Causation (why something happened)
⚠️Business context you haven't provided
⚠️Whether the data itself is reliable
⚠️Business judgment and decisions
⚠️Whether a trend will continue
⚠️ If AI says "sales dropped BECAUSE of..." — that's a red flag. Correlation ≠ causation.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "This is the most important slide in the chapter."] Key talking points: • LEFT column: These are pattern-matching tasks. AI is genuinely good at these. Trust but verify. • RIGHT column: These require context, domain knowledge, and judgment that AI doesn't have. • The big trap: AI will CONFIDENTLY state causation. "Sales dropped because of the price increase" — but it doesn't know if you raised prices. It's just guessing from patterns. • Key rule: if AI uses the word "because" in analysis, treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a fact. • The data reliability point: if someone entered wrong numbers in the spreadsheet, AI can't know that. Garbage in = garbage out. Discussion prompt: "Has anyone had AI make a confident causal claim? What was it?"

The Verification Imperative for Data

Every AI data insight must pass three checks before you trust it:
🧮
1. Verify Calculations
Pick 2-3 numbers AI claims.
Calculate them yourself.
Do they match?
📈
2. Verify Trends
If AI says "increasing trend"—
check the actual numbers.
Is the trend real and significant?
🔍
3. Verify Causation
If AI claims X caused Y—
ask: "Do I have evidence
beyond correlation?"
🎯 Rule: Never present an AI-generated number to a stakeholder without manually checking it first
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's your 3-check system for any AI data output."] Key talking points: • Check 1 — Calculations: AI might say "average revenue is £45,200" — pull out a calculator, add the values, divide. Does it match? • Check 2 — Trends: AI says "revenue grew steadily" — but did it? Maybe it grew in Q1-Q3 then dropped in Q4. "Steady" is subjective. • Check 3 — Causation: The hardest one. AI might say "the new marketing campaign caused the growth" — but it can't know that without experiments. • The stakeholder rule: if you're putting a number in a report, email, or presentation — verify it first. Your reputation depends on accuracy, not AI's. Facilitation tip: Emphasise that this isn't paranoia — it's professionalism. Doctors check test results. Accountants check figures. AI users should too.
