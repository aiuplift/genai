01
The Data You're Feeding AI
What happens after you hit Enter
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Let's start with the uncomfortable truth about where your data goes."] Key talking points: • This chapter reveals what actually happens to data you paste into AI tools • Most people have never thought about this — it feels like a private conversation • It's not. It's a data transfer to an external company.

What Happens to Your Input

💾
Storage
Your text is stored on provider servers — often 30 days minimum. Some providers retain indefinitely for safety reviews.
🧠
Training Use
Free-tier tools often use your inputs for model training. Your client's financials could influence future model outputs.
🌍
Jurisdiction
Data may cross borders. EU client data on US servers? That's potentially a GDPR violation — regardless of your intent.
⚠️ Treat the AI input box like a public billboard — if you wouldn't post it publicly, don't paste it
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Three things happen the moment you hit Enter."] Key talking points: • Storage: your data lives on someone else's server. You don't control retention policies. • Training: free tools use your data to improve. That client name is now in a training set. • Jurisdiction: data crosses borders instantly. EU-originating data on US servers = potential GDPR issue. • The billboard test: if you wouldn't tape it to the office notice board, don't paste it. Discussion prompt: "How many of you use free-tier AI tools for work tasks? Do you know their data retention policy?" Facilitation tip: Don't scare — inform. The point is awareness, not paralysis.

02
The Three-Tier Framework
Classify before you paste
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "So how do we decide what's safe? We need a classification system."] Key talking points: • This framework gives you a decision tool for EVERY paste • Three tiers: Safe, Redaction Required, Avoid Entirely • Simple enough to use in 10 seconds, robust enough for real decisions

The Three-Tier Classification

🟢
SAFE
• Public information (press releases, published reports)
• Generic templates and formats
• General knowledge questions
• Your own creative writing
• De-identified sample data
🟡
REDACTION REQUIRED
• Internal docs (anonymize first)
• Meeting notes (remove names/projects)
• Performance feedback (strip identifiers)
• Client scenarios (generalize details)
• Financial patterns (remove amounts)
🔴
AVOID ENTIRELY
• Passwords, API keys, credentials
• Personal health or medical data
• Legal privileged communications
• Trade secrets and IP
• Unredactable unique scenarios
💡 When in doubt, move UP one tier. Yellow → treat as Red. Green → treat as Yellow.
[TIMING: 5 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's your decision framework. Three tiers, colour-coded."] Key talking points: • GREEN: paste freely. Public data, generic questions, templates. • YELLOW: usable but ONLY after anonymization. Most workplace data lives here. • RED: never paste, even anonymized. Some data is too risky at any level. • The "when in doubt, move up" rule prevents edge-case mistakes • Most people are currently treating YELLOW as GREEN — that's the gap Discussion prompt: "Think about your last 5 AI prompts. How many were genuinely GREEN?" Facilitation tip: Spend time on YELLOW — that's where the practical skill is. RED is obvious once you know it.

03
Making Data Safe
Anonymization techniques that preserve utility
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "So YELLOW data needs redaction. How do we actually do that?"] Key talking points: • This chapter teaches the practical skill of anonymization • The goal: strip identifying info while keeping the task useful • Five techniques that work for any scenario

5 Anonymization Techniques

👤
1. Replace names with roles
"Sarah Chen" → "the team lead" | "Acme Corp" → "the client"
💰
2. Remove or generalize financials
"$2.4M contract" → "a mid-six-figure contract" | "$85K salary" → "mid-range salary"
📅
3. Generalize dates and timelines
"March 15, 2025" → "mid-Q1" | "due December 3" → "due in 6 weeks"
🏢
4. Abstract company details
"our London office expansion" → "office expansion project" | "Project Phoenix" → "the migration project"
🎯
5. Preserve the task
Keep the structure, question, and intent intact. AI doesn't need real names to help you write feedback or analyse patterns.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Five techniques. Simple but powerful."] Key talking points: • Walk through each with the example — show how information is preserved while identity is removed • Technique 5 is the principle: AI doesn't need to know WHO to help you with WHAT • The goal isn't to obscure — it's to remove the link between data and identity • These take 30-60 seconds once you build the habit Discussion prompt: "Which of these techniques would you use most often in your daily work?" Facilitation tip: Relate to their specific roles. Finance → technique 2. HR → technique 1. Operations → technique 4.

Before & After: Sensitive → Safe

talking points for Sarah Chen (EMP-4521),
Senior Analyst in the London office.

What was preserved: Role level, behaviour pattern, rating, the actual task (writing talking points)

Preserved: the actual task and all relevant context. • Time to anonymize: ~30 seconds once you know what to strip Discussion prompt: "Would this anonymized prompt still get you useful output? What's missing?" Facilitation tip: Point out that AI actually performs BETTER with the clean version — less noise, clearer task.

04
The Safe Prompt Pattern
A repeatable 4-step process for every sensitive task
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Now let's turn those techniques into a repeatable process."] Key talking points: • The 5 techniques are skills — this chapter gives you a systematic process • 4 steps, every time, for any sensitive prompt • Takes 30-60 seconds once it becomes habit

The Safe Prompt Pattern

STEP 1
🎯
Identify the Task
What do you actually need AI to help with? Separate the task from the data.
→
STEP 2
✂️
Strip Identifying Info
Remove names, IDs, amounts, dates, locations, project names.
→
STEP 3
🔄
Replace with Generics
Use roles, ranges, and abstractions. Keep the structure intact.
→
STEP 4
✅
Verify Utility
Will AI still produce useful output? If not, add more context (without identity).
⏱️ This takes 30–60 seconds once it's habit. Faster than fixing a data breach.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Four steps. Every time. For any sensitive task."] Key talking points: • Step 1: Most people skip this — they paste everything and let AI figure out what's relevant • Step 2: The mechanical removal. Names, IDs, amounts, dates — if it identifies a person or deal, strip it. • Step 3: Replace with generics that preserve meaning. "The team lead" not just "[REDACTED]" • Step 4: Critical — if you strip too much, AI can't help. Find the balance. • The whole thing: 30-60 seconds. Compare to the hours spent on a data breach response. Discussion prompt: "Which step do you think people skip most often? Why?" Facilitation tip: Step 4 is where judgment lives. Teach them to test: "Would AI still understand the task?"

What Good AI Governance Looks Like

✅
Acceptable Use Policy — Clear rules on which AI tools are approved and for what purposes
✅
Approved Tool List — Enterprise-tier tools with data processing agreements (not free-tier consumer tools)
✅
Data Classification Guide — Organization-specific version of the three-tier framework (like what we learned today)
✅
Incident Response Plan — What to do if sensitive data IS accidentally shared with an AI tool
✅
Training Programme — Regular upskilling (like this course!) so everyone knows the rules and techniques
💡 If your org doesn't have these yet — you now know enough to advocate for them

Regulatory Landscape 2025

🇪🇺
GDPR
• Personal data requires legal basis for processing
• Pasting into AI = data transfer to third party
• Fines up to 4% of global revenue
• Article 33💡 Article 33 requires notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.: 72-hour breach notification
🤖
EU AI Act
• Risk-based classification of AI systems
• Transparency requirements for AI-generated content
• High-risk AI use (HR, finance) has extra obligations
• Effective from 2025 — applies to EU operations
🏛️
Sector Rules
• Finance: FCA/SEC data handling rules
• Healthcare: patient data is always RED tier
• Legal: privilege rules apply to AI communications
• Education: student data requires special handling
💡 You don't need to memorize these — you need to know they exist and that your classification habits keep you compliant

Needs legal basis. • EU AI Act: new regulation, risk-based.
