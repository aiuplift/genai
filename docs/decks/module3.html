03
Blind Spots
Five ways AI meeting minutes fail silently — and how to catch them
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Now we get into the dangerous territory — where AI fails and you won't notice unless you know what to look for."] Key talking points: • These aren't obvious errors — they're SILENT failures • The output looks professional and polished, which makes errors harder to spot Facilitation tip: "This is the chapter that will save your reputation. AI errors in meeting minutes damage credibility fast."

Five Blind Spots in AI Minutes

1️⃣
Attribution errors. AI assigns statements to the wrong person or invents speakers not present.
2️⃣
Invented attendees. AI may add people to the attendee list who weren't actually in the meeting.
3️⃣
Missing context. AI can't know the political context, history, or unspoken dynamics in the room.
4️⃣
Org hierarchy confusion. AI doesn't know who reports to whom or who has authority to decide.
5️⃣
Decisions vs discussions. AI treats "we talked about X" as "we decided X" — the most dangerous error.
⚠️ #5 is the career-ender. Sending minutes that say "team committed to X" when it was only discussed can cause real damage.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "These are the five silent failures. Each one looks professional in the output but is factually wrong."] Key talking points: • Walk through each with a quick example • #1: "Sarah mentioned..." when Marcus actually said it • #2: AI adds "and the full team" when only 4 people were there • #3: AI can't know that the VP hates this project • #4: AI might assign an action to someone who doesn't have authority • #5: THE BIG ONE — "agreed to March deadline" vs "discussed March as a possibility" Discussion prompt: "Has anyone received meeting minutes where something was wrong but looked plausible?" Facilitation tip: Spend most time on #5. It's the most common and most damaging blind spot.

04
Verification
The 4-Check System — your quality gate for AI-generated documentation
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Now you know what goes wrong. Let's build the system that catches it every time."] Key talking points: • This is the systematic approach — not just "read it and hope you notice errors" • 4 checks, under 2 minutes total, catches 95% of issues Facilitation tip: "This system turns verification from 'I'll glance at it' into a reliable, repeatable process."

The 4-Check Verification System

Run these 4 checks on EVERY AI-generated document. Total time: ~90 seconds.

1️⃣
Attribution Check (30s) — Did each person actually say/commit to what the minutes claim?
2️⃣
Completeness Check (20s) — Are any discussed topics or decisions missing from the output?
3️⃣
Accuracy Check (20s) — Are dates, numbers, and specifics correct? No invented details?
4️⃣
Tone Check (15s) — Does the language match the actual certainty level? No upgraded discussions to decisions?
🛡️ Check #1 catches Blind Spots 1-2. Check #4 catches Blind Spot #5 (the commitment trap).
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's your verification system — 4 checks, 90 seconds, catches almost everything."] Key talking points: • Walk through each check with timing — emphasise this is FAST • Attribution (30s): scan every "X said" or "X will" — verify against raw notes • Completeness (20s): were all topics covered? Any missing decisions? • Accuracy (20s): dates, numbers, names — quick factual scan • Tone (15s): "committed to" vs "discussed" vs "proposed" — language precision Discussion prompt: "Which check do you think most people would skip? That's probably the most important one." Facilitation tip: Answer: Tone check. People assume language is fine if facts are right — but tone errors create false commitments.

QA Prompt Technique — Self-Critique

Use these prompts AFTER generating minutes to make AI check its own work:

QA PROMPT 1: Attribution Audit
"Review the minutes you just generated against the raw notes I provided. For each statement attributed to a specific person, confirm it appears in the original notes. Flag any attribution you're uncertain about."
QA PROMPT 2: Commitment Audit
"Check each action item and commitment in the output. For each one, rate your confidence (high/medium/low) that this was actually AGREED vs merely discussed. Flag anything below 'high' confidence."
💡 These prompts catch ~60% of errors automatically. Human review catches the remaining 40%.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's the power move — make AI critique its OWN output before you even start your manual check."] Key talking points: • QA Prompt 1 targets Blind Spots #1 and #2 (attribution, invented attendees) • QA Prompt 2 targets Blind Spot #5 (decisions vs discussions) • These don't replace human verification — they pre-filter the obvious issues • You'll use these in the hands-on exercise Discussion prompt: "Why do you think asking AI to check itself works at all?" Facilitation tip: Answer: it forces re-examination of the original input. The model re-reads your notes with a critical lens instead of a generative one.

Extract Action Items

⏱️ 3 minutes — ask AI to pull out specific commitments

  • 2 Add constraint: "Only include items where someone explicitly agreed to do something. Mark anything uncertain as 'tentative'."
  • 3 Review the table — does each action have a clear owner and deadline?
  • 4 Note which items feel certain vs which feel assumed by AI.

🔨 STEP 3

Write Follow-up Email

⏱️ 3 minutes — draft the email you'd send to attendees
  • 1 Ask AI: "Based on these minutes, write a follow-up email to all attendees summarising key outcomes and next steps."
  • 2 Specify format: 3 sections — Summary, Action Items, Next Meeting. Under 150 words. Professional but warm tone.
  • 3 Add constraint: "Use 'discussed' for items not yet decided. Use 'agreed' only for confirmed commitments."
  • 4 Read the email — would you send this? What needs adjustment?
💡 The language precision constraint:
Forcing AI to use "discussed" vs "agreed" prevents the #1 email error — false commitments going to the whole team.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Step 3 — turn the minutes into a follow-up email. This is the output people actually read."] Key talking points: • The email is what gets sent — it's the highest-stakes artifact • The language precision constraint is CRITICAL: "discussed" ≠ "agreed" ≠ "committed" • Under 150 words forces conciseness — most follow-ups should be scannable • The tone should match how they actually write to their team Discussion prompt: "Would you send this email as-is? What would you change?" Facilitation tip: Most will notice the email sounds "too formal" or "too generic." That's the AI voice problem — fixable with iteration.
🔨 STEP 4

Run QA Self-Critique

⏱️ 4 minutes — make AI audit its own work, then run your 4-check system

  • 2 Use QA Prompt 2: "Rate confidence for each action item: was it agreed or merely discussed?"
  • 3 Now run YOUR 4-Check System manually: Attribution → Completeness → Accuracy → Tone
  • 4 Fix any errors found. How many did AI catch vs how many did YOU catch?
🎯 Success criteria:
After QA, you should have minutes and an email you'd confidently send. All attributions verified, all commitments confirmed.
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Final step — verification. Make AI check itself, then run your own 4-check system."] Key talking points: • This is the step most people will want to skip. DON'T. • AI self-critique catches ~60% of errors. ⚠️ The Commitment Trap

AI's #1 Operational Writing Failure
AI systematically upgrades tentative language into firm commitments.

What was said: "We might need to push the deadline"
What AI writes: "Team agreed to extend the deadline by one week"

What was said: "Can we do a phased launch?"
What AI writes: "Team committed to phased launch approach"
Prevention
Add constraint: "Use 'discussed' for items not confirmed. Only use 'agreed' or 'committed' for explicit decisions."
Detection
In verification, search for "agreed," "committed," "will," "confirmed" — each one needs source verification.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "This is the single most important warning from today's module."] Key talking points: • This pattern causes REAL workplace damage — people get held to commitments they never made • AI does this because it's trained on formal minutes that use decisive language • Prevention: language precision constraints in your prompt • Detection: ctrl+F for "agreed" and "committed" in any AI-generated minutes • This happens in EVERY AI tool — it's not a bug, it's a fundamental pattern Discussion prompt: "Can you think of a scenario where this could cause serious problems at your workplace?" Facilitation tip: Share a concrete story: "Imagine sending minutes saying 'VP approved budget increase' when she only said 'interesting idea, let's discuss next week.'"
