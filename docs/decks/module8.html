AI Image Generation
From text prompts to professional visuals — how it works and when to use it
[TIMING: 0.5 min] [TRANSITION: "Let's start with the most exciting and misunderstood capability: AI image generation."] Key talking points: • This chapter covers how image generation works and best practices for professional use • We'll look at tools, prompt techniques, limitations, and ethics • By the end of this chapter you'll write effective image prompts for real workplace scenarios

How AI Image Generation Works

🎨 Diffusion Models
• Start with random noise (static)
• Gradually remove noise, guided by your text prompt
• Like a sculptor revealing a shape from marble
• Each step brings the image closer to your description
📝 Text-to-Image Process
• Your prompt is converted into mathematical vectors
• AI matches those vectors to visual patterns it learned
• Trained on billions of image-text pairs from the internet
• Creates NEW images — doesn't copy existing ones
🎯 Key insight: AI doesn't "understand" your image — it matches patterns. Specificity in your prompt = better results
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Let's understand what's happening under the hood."] Key talking points: • Diffusion models are the dominant approach (DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion all use variants) • Think of it like starting with TV static and slowly tuning the picture based on your description • The AI learned patterns from billions of images — "sunset over ocean" triggers certain colour patterns, "professional headshot" triggers others • Important: it creates NEW images that look like what it learned — it doesn't retrieve or copy existing images • This means: it CAN'T generate your specific company logo accurately, or replicate a real person's face perfectly Discussion prompt: "What does this mean for accuracy? If it's generating from patterns, can it get precise details right?"

Prompt Engineering for Images

SUBJECT
What's in the image?
"A bar chart showing Q3 revenue growth"
→
STYLE
What aesthetic?
"Flat design, corporate, minimal"
→
COMPOSITION
How is it arranged?
"Centred, white background, landscape"
→
DETAILS
Colours, lighting, mood?
"Blue/teal palette, soft shadows"
🔑 Formula: [Subject] + [Style] + [Composition] + [Details] = Effective image prompt
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Just like text prompts, image prompts need structure."] Key talking points: • Subject: Be specific. "A chart" → vague. "A horizontal bar chart comparing 4 departments" → clear. • Style: Without style guidance, AI picks randomly. Say "flat design" or "photorealistic" or "watercolour" • Composition: Layout matters. Say "landscape orientation" or "centred with white space around edges" • Details: Colours, lighting, mood, text (though AI struggles with text in images) • Key principle: MORE SPECIFIC = BETTER RESULTS. Vague prompts get generic outputs. Example comparison: "Generate a business image" vs "A clean, modern flat illustration of a diverse team collaborating around a whiteboard, corporate blue palette, minimal style, landscape 16:9"

Limitations & Ethics

⚠️ Technical Limitations
• Text in images — often garbled or misspelled
• Hands/fingers — frequently wrong count
• Specific logos — can't reproduce accurately
• Exact data — charts won't show real numbers
⚖️ Ethical Concerns
• Bias — "CEO" generates mostly white males
• Copyright — who owns AI-generated images?
• Deepfakes — never generate real people's faces
• Disclosure — should you label AI images?
🏢 Workplace rule: Use AI images for concepts and placeholders — never pass them off as photographs or real data
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Before we practice, let's be clear about what AI CAN'T do with images."] Key talking points: • Text in images: AI is terrible at it. If you need text on an image, generate the image first, add text in Canva/PowerPoint • Hands: A famous weakness. Models are improving but still often get fingers wrong. • Logos: AI can approximate but never perfectly reproduce a specific logo. Use real logo files. • Bias: Ask for "a nurse" and you'll likely get a woman. Ask for "a CEO" and you'll likely get a man. Be specific about diversity. • Copyright: Legal landscape is evolving. Adobe Firefly is safest (trained on licensed content). Use AI images for internal docs, not client-facing materials without checking your org's policy. • Deepfakes: NEVER generate realistic images of real people. This is a serious ethical and legal issue. Discussion prompt: "What's your organisation's policy on AI-generated images? Do you know?"

The "Slide Factory" Trap

🏭 The Trap
AI generates 40 slides for a 10-minute slot.

Each slide has a title and 5 bullet points.

Every slide looks the same.

The audience is bored by slide 3.
✅ The Fix
1. Start with your MESSAGE, not your slides
What's the ONE thing the audience must remember?

2. Structure BEFORE generating
Plan your arc: Hook → Problem → Solution → Call-to-Action

3. One message per slide
If it has more than one point, split it or cut it.
💡 AI produces QUANTITY. Your job is to enforce QUALITY. Fewer slides = stronger presentation.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "This is the single biggest problem with AI presentations."] Key talking points: • AI's default behaviour: given a topic, it generates LOTS of slides with LOTS of text • This is the opposite of good presentation design • Good presentations: fewer slides, one idea each, visual-heavy, text-light • The fix is simple: decide your structure BEFORE using AI, then use AI to help fill in that structure • "One message per slide" — if you can't summarise the slide's point in 6 words, it's too complex • Real-world example: 10-minute meeting slot = 5-8 slides maximum. AI will generate 25. You must CUT. Discussion prompt: "Have you ever sat through a presentation with too many slides? How did it feel?"

[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Here's the workflow that actually produces good AI-assisted presentations."] Key talking points: • Step 1 - Audience: A presentation for the board is different from one for your team. AI doesn't know your audience. • Step 2 - Key Message: Force yourself to write ONE sentence. "We should approve the budget increase because retention costs less than recruitment." • Step 3 - Story Arc: Not just "list of topics" — an actual NARRATIVE. Hook grabs attention, context sets the scene, etc. • Step 4 - NOW use AI: "I have a 5-slide presentation for the executive team. My key message is X. Here's my arc. Help me write compelling slide titles and suggest one visual per slide." • This approach uses AI as amplifier of YOUR thinking, not as replacement for it.

Choosing the Right Chart Type

📊 Bar Chart
Use for: Comparing categories
"Which department spent most?"
✓ Easy to read, compare, rank
📈 Line Chart
Use for: Trends over time
"How did revenue change month to month?"
✓ Shows direction, patterns, momentum
🥧 Pie Chart
Use for: Parts of a whole (MAX 5 slices)
"What % of budget goes to each area?"
⚠️ Avoid with 6+ categories
🔢 Table
Use for: Precise values, comparisons
"What are the exact numbers?"
✓ When precision > patterns
🎯 Ask: "What QUESTION does my data answer?" — that determines the chart type
[TIMING: 4 min] [TRANSITION: "Now let's talk about choosing the RIGHT visualisation for your data."] Key talking points: • The most common mistake: choosing a chart type because it looks pretty, not because it fits the data • Bar chart = comparison. Line chart = trend. Pie chart = proportion (MAX 5 slices). Table = precision. • The PIE CHART RULE: if you have more than 5 categories, use a bar chart instead. Pie charts with 12 slices are unreadable. • Ask yourself: "What question am I helping the audience answer?" — the answer tells you the chart type. • AI tools will often suggest pie charts for everything. You need to override with better judgement. Discussion prompt: "What's the worst chart you've seen in a presentation? What made it bad?"

Colour Psychology in Business

🔵 Blue
Trust, stability, professionalism. Most common corporate colour. Safe for finance, legal, governance.
🟢 Green
Growth, success, sustainability. Use for positive metrics, "go" signals, environmental topics.
🔴 Red
Urgency, danger, losses. Use sparingly for warnings, negative trends, critical alerts.
🟡 Amber/Yellow
Caution, attention, optimism. Good for "needs review" status, moderate risk indicators.
🟣 Purple
Innovation, creativity, premium. Good for new initiatives, strategy, forward-looking content.
⚪ Grey/Neutral
Calm, background, secondary. Use for context, non-critical data, supporting elements.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Colour isn't just decoration — it communicates meaning."] Key talking points: • Colour carries MEANING in business context. Red on a financial chart means "loss" — even if you meant it as decoration. • The traffic light convention is universal: Red = bad/stop, Amber = caution, Green = good/go • AI image generators often use random colours. You should override with intentional colour choices. • Consistency matters: once you assign blue to "Department A" on slide 3, it must stay blue on slide 7. • Accessibility: ~8% of men are colour-blind. Never rely on colour ALONE to convey meaning — add labels or patterns. • Quick rule: limit to 3-4 colours per visualisation. More than that = visual chaos.

Layout Hierarchy & Whitespace

📐 Visual Hierarchy
• Size: Bigger = more important
• Position: Top-left reads first (in English)
• Contrast: High contrast draws the eye
• Isolation: Space around an element = emphasis
🫧 The Power of Whitespace
• Whitespace is not "wasted" space
• It guides the eye and reduces cognitive load
• Crowded slides = audience stops reading
• Rule: 40% of slide surface should be empty
💡 AI-generated slides are often TOO DENSE. Your edit should usually be: remove elements, add whitespace.
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "Good visual communication follows principles of layout hierarchy."] Key talking points: • Visual hierarchy tells the audience WHERE TO LOOK FIRST. Without it, they scan randomly and miss your point. • The 4 tools: size, position, contrast, isolation. Use them intentionally. • Whitespace: AI-generated presentations often cram every pixel with content. This is the opposite of good design. • The 40% rule: if more than 60% of your slide has content on it, it's too crowded • When you receive an AI-generated slide deck, your first edit should be: identify the ONE key message per slide, and remove everything that doesn't support it • This creates whitespace naturally Discussion prompt: "Think of a billboard you've seen. It probably had very few words and lots of space. Why? Because you have 3 seconds to read it. Slides are the same."

🔄 Create a prompt template — "Every image for this project must include: [style: flat minimal], [palette: brand blues], [composition: landscape 16:9], [mood: professional, warm]"
✅ Quality gate — before using any AI image: Does it match our brand? Would I use this in a client meeting? Does it look consistent with our other materials?
[TIMING: 3 min] [TRANSITION: "How do you keep AI-generated visuals consistent with your brand?"] Key talking points: • The challenge: AI generates varied styles each time. Slide 1 looks photorealistic, slide 5 looks cartoonish. • Solution: Create a PROMPT TEMPLATE that enforces consistency across all generations • Hex codes: AI image tools CAN be guided by specific colours if you specify hex codes in your prompt • Style lock: Pick ONE visual style for your project and stick with it. Don't mix "watercolour" with "3D render." • Quality gate: Every AI-generated asset should pass a "brand consistency check" before going into a final presentation • Practical tip: Save your best prompt as a template. Key Takeaways

🎨 AI generates visuals, not communication. You must judge whether the output actually communicates your message to your specific audience.
📝 Specificity drives quality. Subject + Style + Composition + Details = effective image prompts. Vague prompts = generic results.
🏭 Avoid the slide factory. Structure your presentation (audience, message, arc) BEFORE using AI. AI expands your thinking, doesn't replace it.
📊 Chart type follows the question. What question does your audience need answered? That determines bar, line, pie, or table.
🫧 Whitespace is your friend. AI-generated visuals are often too dense. Edit by removing, not adding.
